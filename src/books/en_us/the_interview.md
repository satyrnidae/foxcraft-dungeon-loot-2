# The Interview

  The room was small, and warmly lit by recessed ceiling fixtures. Along one of the white walls an oak bookshelf stood, housing several slim electronic reading devices. A large tan and brown clay pot sat in a corner opposite the shelves, housing a lush green exotic fern, and on the back wall, a window stood half open, a cool, spring breeze entering the room. In the center of the room sat a large, black desk. On either side of the desk, two men sat in velvet colored chairs. The one on the side closest to the front of the room was dressed in a formal black suit with a red tie. His blond-brown hair was combed to the side, and his blue-green eyes stared out through rectangular spectacles resting on his slender nose. He seemed young, in his late twenties, at most. If this man could be considered a young professional, with his slight figure and sharp suit, the man that sat across from him could be considered the absolute antithesis. His thinning, greying black hair was uncombed, but not messy. With his age placed around fifty, he was much pudgier than the other, and wore a short sleeved, white dress shirt. The black tie around his neck hung loosely. His brown eyes were directed at a sheet of paper on the desk. The younger man fidgeted in his seat as the latter continued to read.

  Finally the older man straightened up, adjusted his tie, and spoke. "Your résumé checks out well," he said in a gruff tone. "I'm just gonna have to run a thorough background check; nothing personal, just company policy." The younger man nodded, twiddling his thumbs.

  The older man tapped the surface of his desk, which immediately woke like a computer from a screensaver. Upon its surface, a keyboard appeared, as well as a clock. A glass pane extended from the desk, upon which was projected the company logo: a stylized waterfowl with outstretched wings and its beak pointed into the sky. The man pressed a quick series of keys. The computer-desk dinged a confirmation tone, and a window appeared on the glass pane. The window displayed a picture of the younger man, along with several lines of text. The older man quickly scanned the text, nodded slightly, and closed the window.

  "You're clean," said the interviewer after a short pause. "The computer'll gauge your potential, and we'll get back to you in a couple of months. Thanks for your time, Mr. Tyler. We look forward to your working here." The interviewer half-stood and extended a hairy arm. Mr. Tyler got up and shook the hand firmly. Nodding slowly, the interviewer sat down one more, and Mr. Tyler, taking the cue to leave, turned and left, the automatic doors closing behind him with a soft hiss.
