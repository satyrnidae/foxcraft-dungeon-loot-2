__<style class=".title"><style class=".symbologie">></style> Prologue <style class=".symbologie"><</style></style>__

   The intrepid adventurer has likely acquainted themselves with the humble Ghast.  Wafting lazily throughout the cavernous bowels of the Nether, these balloonish behemoths strike terror into the hearts of many with their echoing calls and pained wheezes reverberating throughout the caverns.  But aside from their explosive personality, much about the Ghasts remains an enigma.  Where do they come from? How do they multiply?  Find out these wonderful truths and more in...

<style class=".title">Chapter One</style>
<style class=".title"><style class=".symbologie">>-<</style></style>
<style class=".title">Larval Stage</style>
<br>
   The humble Ghast starts its life as a microscopic windborne spore, held aloft by the insipid heat of the magmatic layer of the Nether, lazily searching for a suitable host.  You see, Ghasts begin their life as tiny obligate parasites, which require a host organism to germinate.  The zombified piglin is a perfect candidate, as its rotting flesh belies an utterly ravaged immune system. Unable to fend off the sporatozoa in this state, the piglin is quickly infected.

   It is an old wives tale in piglin circles (of which I am very well acquainted) that touching a zombie piglin will transfer the ghast spore to you.  This is largely unfounded; the spore is not well equipped to fend off even the comparatively primitive immune system of a piglin, and contracting one will lead to little more than a mild fever and perhaps some congestion.  (And, as an aside, the piglins would do well to worry more about the potential transmission of the zombification curse than the ghast spore.)

   For the zombie piglin, however, one the infection begins it is all but over.  Soon, the spore will find its way into the zombie hog's brainstem, worming its way deep into what remains of its host's central nervous system.  There, it will entice the piglin to throw itself bodily into the lava lakes below, where the next step of the ghasts lifecycle will, quite literally, take root.
<br>
<vcenter>
<style class=".title">Chapter Two</style>
<style class=".title"><style class=".symbologie">>-<</style></style>
<style class=".title">Polyp Stage</style>
<br>
   Once its quite unfortunate host has sunken into the depths of the Nether's lakes, the second stage of the Ghast's lifecycle begins.  I have been fortunate enough to have come into possession of enough fire resistance potions to examine this process in its entirety, thanks in no small part to a helpful cleric in Yoldur's Folly.  Shortly after reaching the lake's bottom, thin, hairlike tendrils will erupt from the limp body of the piglin, searching for a soft vein of soul sand or soil to latch onto.  It slowly pulls itself down to the loam, attaching itself quite firmly.  Over the course of a week, the skin of the piglin bleaches, bulges, and ruptures as a soft, bone white fruiting body erupts from the ravaged form. I am reminded, somwhat morbidly, of puffed corn.

   The polyp grows over the course of about a month, ballooning to the size of a small house, with long feeding tentacles splayed around a gaping maw ringed with teeth.  Any unfortunate striders who approach too close to the polyps grasp are entangled and engulfed whole.

   When the polyp has finally sated itself, it will begin the process of budding, and eventually, a new Ghast separates from the polyp, ready to float and terrorize the inhabitants of the Nether.
<br>
<vcenter>
<style class=".title">Chapter Three</style>
<style class=".title"><style class=".symbologie">>-<</style></style>
<style class=".title">Reproductive Stage</style>
<br>
   Of course we cannot complete this book without revealing how this all begins.  How does a Ghast release those undeadly sporatozoa?  Ghast reproduction is entirely asexual.  A ghast will build an explosive shell within its body, with harvested sulfur and carbon from soul sand fields and skeletons, and infuse the shell with thousands upon thousands of tiny spores.  When a ghast sees a suitable host, or a sufficiently threatening invader, it will launch the shell bodily towards the offending party, bursting into flame and a flurry of spores upon contact. Yes, that is correct: ghasts pelt you with their eggs while you explore the nether!
<br>
<vcenter>
<style class=".title">Chapter Four</style>
<style class=".title"><style class=".symbologie">>-<</style></style>
<style class=".title">Death of the Polyp</style>
<br>
   Ghast Polyps are hardy creatures, and generally do not find themselves in much danger most of the time, but it is possible for the tides of the Nether's lava seas to shift, and for great upheavals to force the anchored Polyp out into the open air.  An exposed polyp is not long for this world; much like the striders, they require the red heat of magma to sustain their infernal internal processes, and in its stressed state the polyp quickly burns itself out, leaving nothing behind but ash and their calcinated scaffolding, sticking out of the ground like large ribcages in soul sand fields.

   It is under these scaffolds that the intrepid adventurer may find the only part of the polyp that can survive such a transition:  an underdeveloped medusa can sometimes be deposited during the pyrrhic death of a polyp.  Unfortunately for the medusa, what little water it contains is driven forth from its body, leaving it a husk deposited on the floor.

   Some say that you may be able to take the discarded and dehydrated medusae back to the Overworld and soak them in water or milk to rehydrate them, but I fear that may be asking for trouble.  One cannot know the mind of a Ghast, and it remains to be seen if one would be happy to be taken far from its home like this.
