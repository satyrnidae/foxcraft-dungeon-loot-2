<br>

`A`drop dventures do often begin with a young man, a nobody, a conduit through which the reader may associate himself or herself with the world of the story.  This, I think, would be a mistake on my part to begin my story as such.  This is because our story does not begin, but end with a young man, a nobody.  It is this end that I will attempt to explain, through my too-few powers of storytelling and by my personal relationship with said young man, and by such, hope to regale you with how he came to be.
<br>

## Chapter I
<style class=".title">A Good Story</style>


  Good stories begin at the beginning, but seeing as this story is not particularly pleasant at the start, let us begin at the end.  The room was dark, dank, and smelled much like its former occupants (who I will decline to name at this time, but whom I assure you will later be of some singular importance).  The room was also small.  Large by my standards, myself being a very small man, but small to the giants that once dined there.

  Being once a dining room, at the center of the circular chamber sat, or rather slumped, an oaken table, its legs worm-eaten and rotted, and its surface stained with the wine, meaty juices, and blood that once flowed across its waxed top.  From my vantage point quite far below the table, I could see with my one free eye, the cracks and fissures that spiderwebbed along the bottom of the table.  Countless amateur autographs made grotesque constellations, splattered about the underside of the dining table in the angular and sharp scrawl of the giants.

  At no point, however, could I fully appreciate the mundane reality of the world around me, as my frantic running and constant attempts to avoid falling over my crippled leg and be sent sprawling into a heap of ancient left-overs.  The true horror of my surroundings, which I will spare you here, did not come to light in the darkness, but rather as I came to an unexpected stop, and finally fell.

  I find that it is in the moment, the single instant between standing and falling, between calmly walking upright, and being lain to the floor like an old rug that the mind works the quickest and the eyes, or in my case, eye, sees the sharpest.  It was at this moment, that the world around me closed quite suddenly in a manner that I would not deduce until some time later.  It was also at this moment that every door in the room, fourteen in number, burst open much like a gaseous bubble that rises from a pot of boiling liquid.

  The wooden doors, strapped with iron, were simultaneously and instantly rended apart, leaving only gelatinous waste behind.  Through these doors flowed black-robed giants like ooze from a weeping wound.  These were my final images in the moment before my madness that brought me to my story, a good story.

  Our story.

  THE story.
