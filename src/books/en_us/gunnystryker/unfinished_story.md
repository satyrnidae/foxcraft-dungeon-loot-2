# Chapter I

   "Magic ain't for no man that can fight with his own two hands!" exclaimed the man gruffly as he flexed a massive arm with a flourish and slapped the bulging muscle.

   The other men laughed loudly and racuously through the tavern.  It was a large room, for a village this small.  Several square wooden tables splayed about the room with three or four hulking men chuckling over ale at each.  Animal furs decorated every inch of the floor; skins of bears or big cats or wolves created a thick carpet from wall to wall.  This was the men's hall:  a place for warriors and scholars and beggars alike to join and trade stories of adventure.  Great beams a pace wide held aloft a ceiling of slatted wooden plates.  Rain rattled on the roof quietly as the men spoke.

   "There ain't no magic any man can do that will do anythin' I can't with my arms," the man spoke again, softer this time, not bragging but simply instructung a poorly educated youngling.  The man was beastly; with the neck of an ox and arms to match, he could easily best any other of the soldiers in the den.  His beard grew ragged as it reached his chest, stopping short of his breast.  The crown of his head was bare, completely bald in the style of the area.  He wore a leather jerkin with large iron disks stamped with an intricate crest.  Each shone as if they had been newly polished and the leather showed no cracks or wear.  The sleeves of his green shirt underneath were rolled to the elbows and he wore a wide belt of woven brass about his waist.  Piercing blue eyes sunk deep into a round face that betrayed his gentle tone.

   Margil sat quietly in his chair, his mousey face and narrow chin resting on a large leather-bound folio, with a bronze clasp holding it shut.  "But you must agree, Master Tern," Margil said tentatively, gesturing toward the hulking man with a finger, "that in Temorin, Arinfella, Ostagath, and even here in Ustangrall, magic has been a strong part of the heritage."  He looked at Master Tern carefully, never quite meeting his eyes, and shifted uncomfortably in the too-tall chair.
