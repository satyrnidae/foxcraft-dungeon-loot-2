title: Carbon Valley
author: Isa Massacre
generation: tattered
rarity: common
---

  Welcome, West Virginians, to the jewel of the Appalachias, the center of industry, the up and comin' town of Carbon Valley, West Virginia!

  …is prolly what I woulda said about fifteen years ago, in the early sixties, if I coulda strung together a sentence that complex as a three year old whelp.

  You see, back before the anthracite veins stopped yieldin' a profitable margin for this lil' minin' town's profiteers, Carbon Valley really was a jewel, at least in comparison to some of those company owned ventures out there. We had a mayor, and a home owner's association, hell even a general store what took real, cold, hard American dollars. From what my granddaddy (God rest his soul) told me, they tried that company scrip nonsense, but the town was here long before the minin' company ever set its greedy eyes on the heart of our mountain, an' it sure as hell will be here long after.

  How long? Well, prolly not much longer, 'f'I'm bein' ernest. When Foucault Granite and Marble showed up in '68 we were already on our last legs. Funding was dryin' up faster'n the damn coal did. Foucault offered an out: come work for them, and they'd fund the continued existence of our little slice of Appalachia.

  In short order the hot medical craze shifted from black lung to silicosis, and even though just ten years ago the Scar was but a twinkle in some gravel executive's eye, now it carves a huge swath through the long-before-untouched forests on the side of ol' Blue. If a mountain could bleed, we'd be drownin'.

  That fundin'? It never really came. That was pitched by the Mayor more'n the company, an' while he drives around down town shakin' hands from the window of his fancy Firebird Trans Am, the rest of the town is better budgeted to own ponies rather'n pony cars.

  Why stay? Askin' that question's gonna get you a lot of angry stares out here. Nobody leaves Carbon Valley. Everybody livin' here can trace their lineages back through the heyday of the mining enterprise. Hell, a few can go back to the homesteadin' and charcoal days, back when Carbon Valley earned its name. Concurrent cycles of boomin' and bustin' so reliable the people here will stake their lives and livelihood on it.

  But e'er since Foucault came 'round, somethin's changed. The woods which welcomed our fathers and their father's fathers since time immemorial have grown dark, forebodin'. Always feels like somethin's watchin' ya, just beyond the edge of the trees. Folks've gone missing, they say. Only one or two a year, but, one day they're eating at Chuck's Diner downtown, and the next it's like they ne'er existed. Like the woods themselves reached out and swallowed them up, lives and families whole.

  You might just think they finally had it with the dead end town an' a dead end job and a death sentence pilin' up in their lungs but if there's one thing I need you to remember it's this:

  Nobody leaves Carbon Valley.
