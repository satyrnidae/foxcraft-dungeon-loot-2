# Lost Focus

  "Kylie? Are you listening?"

  Kylie, who had been lost in thought, snapped to attention and winced. This was the third time this month she had been daydreaming during an important meeting, and definitely not the first time her manager had to snap her focus back. "Y-yes, Harry, thank you," she replied sheepishly, "What... What were you saying again?"

  Harry sighed as he put his hand to his face. Not a good sign. Clearly she and him were going to have a chat about it after the meeting. It definitely wasn't the first time they would have that chat either. "As I was saying..."

  Kylie tried to fix her attention to whatever Harry was droning on about but it kept slipping away like sand through her fingers. Her mind was often elsewhere, but her grandfather's passing had made focusing on anything for more than a few hours much more difficult than she had even thought possible. She'd found herself thinking back to her childhood with him several times this past week alone.

  She noticed Harry was looking directly at her again, and she realized that she'd let her focus slip yet again. Giving a start she frantically tried to catch up with her notes, feeling her face grow red until Harry looked away.

  Harry sighed again as he stood up. The rest of the team had shuffled off to lunch, but Kylie stayed behind.

  "Uh, Harry. I'm..." she started, but the words caught in her throat.

  Harry held up a hand to stop her anyways. "It's okay, Kylie. I get it." He arranged the various timetables and project design documents into a neat pile before slipping them into his slim loose-leaf binder and snapping the rings shut. "Losing a grandparent can be difficult; I know that."

  Kylie didn't say anything in return, averting her eyes from the plump man.

  After a poignant pause, Harry continued. "How about you take the rest of the day off?" he suggested. "It's Friday, so I'm sure the team will manage."

  Kylie's embarrassment flared. "I- no, that's not necessary!" she insisted, though, to be honest, it was an extremely tempting offer. "I'm fine, really. I'm fine."

  Maybe if she said it enough she could convince herself, but Harry wasn't falling for it. He slid the trapper-keeper over the conference table. "Here, review these when you can. Peter can help give you any context you need, since you didn't seem to get much from this meeting. Take your time. I'll see you Monday."

  Harry left the room, and Kylie was left sitting in silence. She should have been grateful of Harry's understanding, and willingness to accommodate, she figured. So why did she feel so much worse?

  Kylie didn't even stop for lunch on the way home. Instead, she took a meandering path back on the city bus lines. The train would have been quicker, and more direct, but Kylie didn't want quick or direct. Not right now, when going home felt more like punishment than a relief.
