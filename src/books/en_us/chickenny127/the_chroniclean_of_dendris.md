## The Inception of Dendris

<vbottom>
---
<br>

`A`drop nd then it began. <style class=".divinity">Dendris</style> rose from the void and created the cube, the second great blessing. At the center was a core of nothingness. Nothingness was wrapped in a blanket of hard stone, black and strong. Lava ran through it, and lit up the night. Another layer of stone coated over the waves of lava, leaving magma pooling underneath in tight bundles. The heat turned the dull stone into the plans for the future. Waves of water crashed onto the surface, pulled into the cube as the will of <style class=".divinity">Dendris</style> commanded. The water seeped down into the magma, and expanded, creating the dark deep caverns that are riddled with hidden secrets, heat driving their creation. The water soaked into the land, and <style class=".divinity">Dendris</style> thought it good.

  The final blessing, life upon the surface of the cube. Fish sprang into the rivers and oceans. Rabbits flooded the desert. Goats roamed the high mountains. The cube was filled with life.

  Into this world, <style class=".divinity">Dendris</style> added their greatest creation: Ifs. The Ifs looked to the trees, the sand, and began to create. Ifs created homes, shelter from the rain, tools to manage the land, all for their benefit. Some Ifs saw <style class=".divinity">Dendris</style>, and asked for a source of food that could provide all they might hope for. Something small, weak, and pitiful that might provide sustenance.

  <style class=".divinity">Dendris</style> looked at all that had been created, and thought this request disturbing. There were rabbits, but the Ifs declared them too fast, and did not want to chase after them. The fish were plentiful, but it took time to fish, and the Ifs got wet. The goats were high in the mountain, and climbing was too tiring. <style class=".divinity">Dendris</style> saw this, and refused, claiming that the Ifs were forsaking the purpose of creation.

  <style class=".divinity">Dendris</style> looked at those who had stood uncomfortably by as this occurred. While these Ifs came in robes of bright colors and demanded better food, many stood by. These Ifs wore simple rags. Their shorn clothes dangling off their thin bodies, desperate for food in a world of plenty.

  In this moment, <style class=".divinity">Dendris</style> saw the Great Challenge, and came upon the course. <style class=".divinity">Dendris</style> submitted to the request of fools, and fools are parted from their gifts. The fools were turned into the first pigs. Worse still, they were scattered across the world. And with the scattering, a curse.

  They had failed in their first life, and so they would be forced to take a second. A life as a pig would be followed by a life of torment. Their punishments would vary, but all would be terrible. Upon death, some would shed their skins while others would lose skin and flesh, cursed to burn in the sun, tormented until their deaths. Those who seek comfort would find it to be their end, as they explode into those they seek comfort from. Many other torments would come to be, and <style class=".divinity">Dendris</style> would create a world for them. A world of flame, with only the most tenuous of connections to the great cube. There would be no peace for those who abandoned the gifts of <style class=".divinity">Dendris</style>.

  Those who stood idly by faced a new hazard. <style class=".divinity">Dendris</style> did not curse them, for they did nothing wrong. These Ifs did nothing right. They were punished for failing to act. As such, their curse was to deal with the consequences of their actions. These new tortured souls would come for them, and dealing with it was their problem, one which <style class=".divinity">Dendris</style> would offer no assistance. This was their fate, to be haunted by their failure forever. Thus, the cube was made, and all that is known is known. Thus is the will of <style class=".divinity">Dendris</style>.

## The Iron and the Giant

<vbottom>
---
<br>

`S`drop calito was a young If, wandering through the fields of flowers near their sunny town. After the harvest had finished, seeing the far side of the mountain was finally a priority. None from the town went near the location, so Scalito needed to see it.

  Scalito saw some movement among the brush. After a flash of sweat, Scalito calmed, for it was still bright this day, with the sun still lingering in the sky. Scalito stepped through the brush, and spotted an orange shape. A green stem in its back, ridges of bright orange stood out against the grass it grew from.

  Scalito was joyful at finding such a nice thing, and began to speak to it. Scalito told it stories of the village, of the past, of the present, and of hopes and dreams. Scalito talked long, and wondered much why to tell this pumpkin so much.

  Scalito did not stop until a groan and rustling of brush broke the mood. Scalito looked up and saw a dark sky, behind and saw rotting flesh. As Scalito started to run, he tripped over the pumpkin, kicking it down the slope they stood on. The pumpkin rolled down to a small cave, which Scalito followed it into. Scalito sealed the cave shut and curled up. Only a few bits of wood and a pumpkin, there was no choice but to wait for the dawn, to hope the sun would dispel the dangers.

  As Scalito settled in for the long night, the pumpkin rolled over and demanded an apology. Scalito threw a stick and screamed, but seeing that this changed nothing, picked up the stick and continued screaming. The screaming also failing to change anything about the situation, Scalito apologized to the pumpkin. The pumpkin began telling stories of the things it had seen. The pumpkin too had dreams and hopes. It longed to move, to walk on its own, without waiting for the benefit of an If to kick it down a hill for a change of scenery. The pumpkin made a deal with Scalito: In exchange for a body, the pumpkin would remove the monster from outside the cave. Scalito was with no reason to refuse, and so agreed.

  The If spent much time trying to build a body. One of sticks was too flimsy, and cracked under the weight of the pumpkin. One of stone could hold the weight, but was so rigid the pumpkin could not move it. Finally, Scalito build a body of iron, and the pumpkin walked forward. In celebration, Scalito carved a face into the pumpkin, so others could see the joy of the pumpkin.

  Finally, they opened the entrance of the cave, and there was no monster. The sun had come, and the monster was no more in the morning light. Scalito looked to the pumpkin, unsure of what to do. The pumpkin grinned, and walked into the distance, and Scalito walked back to the village and said nothing of his adventure.

  Many years passed, and Scalito grew old. There were many young Ifs who lived in the village, and they had a bad tendency to wander far. Whenever they stayed out too long, they always made it home the next day. Some spoke of a loud noise in the bushes, the sounds of fighting.

  Some spoke of a flash of orange and a call to run. Some would not speak at all, but were not afraid. And Scalito knew. And smiled.

# Fire and Fear

<vbottom>
---
<br>

`V`drop erna had heard of the black gate behind the mountain. Sometimes strange creatures would come from within and do business with the Ifs in the village. The items were foreign and strange, but most of all intriguing. Verna had been warned about the dangers. The traders would speak in hushed tones about the fear beyond the portal. Some were calling for it to be closed. Verna saw potential in exploring new realms. Despite warnings from Cile the Elder, Verna went through the portal to the realm of fire.

  In the realm of fire, Verna was surrounded by smoke and stone. Verna decided to explore the area, and found many odd things which could be brought back to the village. Verna collected many wonderful things, and went back to the village. The villagers were surprised that Verna had found so many items, and asked many questions. Verna did not tell them the truth, and kept their travels a secret.

  Verna went into the land of fire a second time. The fire did not scare them, and they went through, collecting more items to bring to the village. Verna spotted a strange ore, a material that had not been brought by the traders before. Verna saw opportunity for creation in this finding.

  Without warning, a skeleton coated in smoke carrying a sword attacked. Verna was not prepared, and fell to the ground. Before the skeleton could strike, a shield appeared between them. Cile was there, and defeated the smoky skeleton. Cile had seen through Verna's lies, and decided to follow Verna to protect them. Cile brought Verna back to the village, and Verna felt ashamed.

  Verna was shunned in the village. They did not like that Verna had gone into the realm of fire. After this continued for a while, Verna approached Cile. Verna wanted to go back into the portal. Cile did not like this plan. There was danger there, and Cile saw no reason to charge into it. Verna was adamant of the opportunity, and was persistent. Verna told Cile about the strange ore. Finally, Cile agreed to go back.

  The two of them went into the realm of fire, and began searching for the strange ore. Cile kept an eye out for the danger, while Verna searched for the ore and provided backup. Verna collected strange ore, and after many long days in the realm of fire they returned to the village. The villagers were not happy about this, but they tolerated Cile's decision.

  After a few weeks, Verna made a discovery. By merging the strange ore with gold, they were able to create a sturdy metal, far stronger than anything else. The items were lighter, sharper, and more durable than any other equipment. The village was elated with their new tools, and used them regularly. The villagers thanked Verna, and celebrated their new great tools. Cile worked with Verna, and any time the village needed more material, they went into the realm of fire together.

# The Blaze and The Frog

<vbottom>
---
<br>

`A`drop  blaze was sitting on the edge of a riverbank. A frog did not notice and came near. When the blaze spoke, the frog began to flee, but the blaze cried out.

  "Do not run. I need your help. I cannot cross the river. Would you carry me?"

  The frog stopped, and replied,

  "I am certain I could carry you across the river. But why should I trust you? You might kill me."

  "Ahh," the blaze replied, "but in doing so, I would perish in the waters! I would not do something so foolish."

  "Then why not kill me once you're on the other side?" The frog asked, thinking ahead.

  "I could have killed you before now, when you did not see me. I did not, so you can trust me."

  "How can you trust me though?" The frog asked. "At any moment, I could dive beneath the surface, and you would die."

  "Yes, but then I would kill you before you could escape. We will both have to trust each other to make it across the river alive."

  The frog thought on this, and did not have a good reply. As such, the frog agreed to carry the blaze across the river. The blaze climbed aboard, and they floated across the river.

  Neither creature survived the crossing.

  Still, <style class=".divinity">Dendris</style> gives us our blessings, and protection from Mysteria and the strength not to rely on the false powers of the sun. The blessings are scattered deep in the dungeons of the world.

  Someday, <style class=".divinity">Dendris</style> will strike into the back of Mysteria, and the deep will reclaim the sky. The fields will burn, the portals of flame will reopen, and the three headed beast will be slain until light rises into the sky. Only then can <style class=".divinity">Dendris</style> strike down the false gods that have taken our beautiful world.
