# Shall We Play a Game?
<center class=".subtitle">departure</center>

<vbottom>

<center class=".title">by</center>
<center class=".title">Groggus Everus</center>
<br>

`A`drop t the offset of my first journey into the Caverns of Limbo, I was quite unaware of what to expect.

  I had of course spent days on end preparing for the departure, poring over the oldest of my many tomes with the vigour of a much younger man (this, I must admit, being the only action I can take with such zest, given my old age). I followed the instructions detailed in the mustiest of my collection of works, and gathered up the necessary materials.

  I procured from the ceiling of the deathly Nether ample amethysts, and dug from my chests an eye of ender from my adventuring days. My scholar friend Eventius Valadon then aided me in the fashioning of enchanted glass from the orb and sheets of mundane glass. I had a goldsmith forge for me the handle and frame in which the circular, reflective surface was set, and affixed one of my amethysts to the top.

  What a marvelous mirror we had created! It gleamed with magical energy, drawing the eyes of myself and Valadon to its face. Then, as according to the book, the world seemed to pulsate, and our surroundings changed in an instant. No longer were we standing in my friend's basement, staring at cobblestone and wooden walls. No longer did the sun shine through wrought iron decorative windows. No, we had rematerialized in Limbo, far beneath the surface world.

  This place would be pitch black, but for the abundant orbs of golden light shining across the Asphodelic plains. Beasts of a kind I had never dreamed to encounter roamed across the broken tracts of dark grass. There were cows with great fins on their backs and bobbing antennae. There were hogs of alien nature and squids resplendent with crimson bioluminescence. These were but a few of the amazing creatures I noticed in the first moments of my arrival, and I noted them all down in my "Bestiary of Unexpected Things."

  Our marvel at the existence of this beautiful world lasted for quite some time, but it was brought to a sudden end when Valadon directed my attention toward an approaching animal, some form of chicken with the tail of a scorpion, and the monster following behind it.
