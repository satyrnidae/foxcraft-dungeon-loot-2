<span class=".title">A Story by Captain Zephyrr</span>

<span class=".title">Edited by Isa Massacre</span>
<!-- page-break -->

Falden seated the man before the fire, attempting to ward away the frost that clung to traveler's cloak.

"May I ask why you were journeying through Asher's Glen in such wintry weather?" Falden inquired, passing the time while the other man thawed out. When the traveler didn't answer, Falden went over to get his guest something warm to eat and drink.

Finally, in a hoarse voice, almost a whisper, the traveler replied, "I had not accounted for the blizzard. I thank you very much for dragging me out of it."

"Ah, the blizzard. You aren't the only one who has been caught unawares by such tumultuous weather. Many a man has perished while traveling on the coldest of nights, and many of them were not simple fools," Falden said grimly, returning to the traveler with a tray of steaming chicken breasts, then lightened his voice. "So, we have not been properly introduced yet! I am Falden, proprietor of this humble tavern. What is your name, and where were you headed in such fierce weather, friend?"

The traveler grimaced slightly at the word "friend", but he answered anyway, "I am Ltwe of Foxtrot. I was traveling to Thalbodir. I hear it is a wondrous sight indeed."

Falden paused, setting down the food with a soft thunk, and leaned in close. "Thalbodir, friend? That's to the southwest — and the far southwest, too. Maybe a day's journey from where you started, but from here? A week, at best. I am afraid that you have been moving steadily northward."

"Northward? No, no, surely I was going East!" Ltwe shifted his pack and withdrew a small pocket compass, unfastening its clasp. "My father's compass cannot lie to me — that would be absurd!"

Falden looked at the compass in the man's outstretched arm. Sure enough, the compass's northern arrow was frozen in its rotation, pointing approximately West. "I learned at a tender age how to navigate by reading the stars," Falden murmured, "and I know for certain that the East your compass reads is in actuality North."

Ltwe's face betrayed his intrigue. For a second, Falden thought to regale him with a tale from his days of adventure, long ago… but he shook the memory of the open road from his head, remembering his vow. He instead turned the conversation back to Thalbodir. "You have heard right, traveler, about Thalbodir. It is indeed a sight to behold. A massive, beautiful wall and an oasis larger than any I have ever seen in a desert." He leaned over the mantelpiece, the fireplace casting a long shadow over his face. "It's been a long while since I've visited its shores."

Ltwe perked up, his worries forgotten for a moment. "You have visited Thalbodir?"

"Aye," replied the innkeeper. "A long, long time ago." Falden found himself reminiscing anyway. "Back when I was an adventurer for hire, I visited all the landmarks: the great ruins of Deinotown, the outpost at Pity's Sake, the docks at Silverton, the cold nights in the Valley of Peace…" He chuckled as he cast his gaze wistfully back through the years. "In the ages since my last visit, I'm sure old Thalbodir's got even newer surprises in store."

"Hearing you tell of it makes it sound amazing — I cannot wait to glimpse it myself!" the traveler exclaimed, and a sparkle returned to his eye for a moment before it quickly faded. "Now, though, I suppose I shan't. I am clearly no navigator, and I fear that I am too far off the path to do anything but retrace my steps, if even that." He closed his pocket compass with a despondent sigh, and turned his gaze to the fireplace.

Falden drew the chair across from Ltwe, and slowly lowered himself into it. The wood creaked in protest; the chairs in the tavern were all too small for his considerable bulk. "Why are you so dead set on visiting Thalbodir, my friend?" he asked gently.

The man across from him was silent. For a moment, the only sounds in the tavern were the wind rattling the casement windows and the gentle crackling of the fireplace.

An eternity seemed to pass in an instant before Ltwe replied. "My father," he spoke in a hushed tone. His grip on the compass tightened. "He's grown old, and very sick. Has been for a while now. Copse's Fever is treatable, but it is still a creeping death sentence if he can't get his medicine." His breath caught in his throat as his gaze deepened into the embers. "We— well, my father… he has a contract with an herbalist in Thalbodir. We are endlessly grateful to her, as her medicines have extended my father's time on this earth by more than tenfold, but…" He gripped the compass even tighter as his gaze darkened. "Recently, the medicine has become less effective, and the shipments have become unreliable."

It was a story Falden had heard so often from the travelers who visited his tavern in the warmer months — "Bandits?"

His guest's eyes flashed in the firelight as he turned to face his host. "Yes."

Falden thought back to Ltwe, stumbling half delirious from the cold. He'd found him while returning from the firewood house, and when Ltwe saw Falden emerging from the glen he had brandished an iron shortsword, yelling against the howling winds. "It would have been a death sentence," Falden said lowly, glancing at the blade propped up by the door, "if you'd actually met a bandit on your trip instead of me."

"And it would be a death sentence for my father if I had not tried." Falden had no retort for that. Ltwe's gaze had grown intense, but it wasn't focused on Falden anymore. His grip on his compass had become so tight that his hand was shaking and his knuckles white.

Falden reached up and enveloped the hand in his own, and Ltwe gave a start as he snapped back to the moment. "It's alright, my friend; you're safe now." Ltwe's grip on his compass loosened as Falden gave him a soft smile and lowered his hand. Ltwe blinked and seemed to bristle for a moment, but averted his gaze back to the fire, pulling his hood further forward over his face.

The wooden chair protested again as Falden rose from it to stoke the shimmering flame. It was another long moment before Ltwe spoke. "Why did you stop? Adventuring, that is."

It was Falden's turn for his gaze to falter.

"I uh," he said haltingly, "I made a vow, when my daughter was born. I hung up my leather jerkin and adventurer's pack and settled down here in Asher's Glen Opened this inn." He fished a chain out from under his shirt and unclasped the attached locket.

"Is she…" Ltwe started.

Falden shook his head silently as he stared into the locket. The young girl immortalized within stared back from her father's shoulder, her eyes full of joy, auburn hair shining in the golden sunlight. A beautiful spark of infinite potential and boundless joy, extinguished far too long before her time. It had always been her dream to see all the places her father told her about in his stories.

"No," Falden said finally. "No. Not for a long time." He gently reclasped the locket and let it fall to his chest.

The two men fell silent again. Ltwe picked his compass up and began toying with it, no doubt trying to unstick the needle within.

Falden studied the younger man for a bit. He couldn't be much older than twenty. *The same age Vera would be,* thought the old adventurer ruefully. His gaze left his visitor and wandered around the empty tavern. By this point in the year, travel through Asher's Glen was infrequent, and the inn often sat empty, except for the old adventurer stoking the fire and keeping the taps clean. The emptiness struck Falden far more deeply this night.

"Hells," cursed the old adventurer, and Ltwe gave a start. "You still want to see Thalbodir, yes?"

Litwe slipped his compass back into his pack, stammering, "Well, y-yes, more than anything, but… I'll never be able to find my way there with a broken compass and a howling storm."

"You will if I have any say in it!" Falden turned to Ltwe, grinning like a man possessed. He thought of his daughter as he rested a hand on Ltwe's shoulder.

"I could not ask—" the traveler began to protest, but he was cut off by Falden's cry of "Yet I will answer nonetheless!"

The former adventurer smiled broadly, seeing the acceptance in the traveller's eyes. "Make yourself at home, my friend Ltwe! I will be your guide on this journey, and, if you wish, we shall leave on the morn! Thalbodir awaits!"
